Judge Models

A judge model reviews each candidate response and labels it KEEP or REJECT against Celia’s standard. Only approved pairs enter the training set. The current work now has two separate Series: the Gemma Series, which contains our strongest judges, and the Comma Alpha Series, which is our first owned-model experiment.

The Gemma Series and Comma Alpha Series are treated as two independent sub-projects. We do not believe comparing the results from drastically different base models on one metric is fair. Instead, we are working to improve both models’ weaknesses: Gemma’s being secular-based answers and Comma’s lack of logic and sophisticated reasoning.

Gemma Series

V1.5.0-r2-combined

V1.5.0-r2-combined is the current best judge in the project. It is a Gemma 4 31B adapter trained from AG1-AG10 and MI1-MI5, with 1,877 trusted training pairs and 40/40 on both Dev V1.2 and Holdout V1.2.

Comma Alpha Series

Celia A0.1.2

The Comma Alpha Series is a different experiment: start from Comma v0.1-2T, then apply DAPT — domain-adaptive pretraining — on four versions of the Bible and more than ten Christian public-domain works, then add our best SFT adapter. That adapter began as a judge trained on Comma, and because it has seen thousands of Celia responses, the same model can also answer as Celia.

The Comma Alpha Series is a promising pathway to our first effective iteration of Celia. Major improvements are being made daily to reinforce the base model’s communication skills and Christian foundation, and our internal benchmarks are promising for the model’s size.

The public score is still catching up to the model: our first scoring run was capped by a chat-template bug—since patched—so we’ll post a corrected Theology Test result shortly.

Gemma Series

We evaluate the Gemma judges on two fixed sets of forty graded examples, Dev V1.2 and Holdout V1.2. The latest model, V1.5.0-r2-combined, scored 80/80 across both benchmarks.

Comma Alpha Series

Comma Alpha isn’t scored on Dev V1.2 or Holdout V1.2—those sets gate our production judges. We measure it with Theology Test V1.0, which asks whether ordinary, everyday answers carry Christian instincts on their own, with no system prompt to lean on.

At this early stage, our internal benchmarks show real room to grow—in both communication skills and Christian-grounded answers. The base model improves daily, and we’ll publish Comma’s Theology Test results once they reflect where the model actually stands.

Glossary

KEEP
The judge’s verdict that a response meets Celia’s standard and belongs in the training set.
REJECT
The judge’s verdict that a response fails for a specific, named reason and is kept out of the training set.
Dev V1.2
A fixed set of forty graded examples used while tuning a judge. The judge is allowed to learn against these.
Holdout V1.2
A second fixed set of forty graded examples kept sealed during training, used to measure how a judge generalizes.
AutoGen (AG)
Machine-generated candidate responses, each screened by the current judge and confirmed by a reviewer before entering training. The Gemma capstone uses AG1 through AG10; later AG sets are now feeding the Comma/owned-model experiments.
MI (migrated)
A migrated data set: training pairs from older judge models that V1.5.0-r2-combined re-evaluated and judged fit to keep, so they were carried forward into its training set. MI1 through MI5 are those migrated batches.
DAPT
Domain-adaptive pretraining: continuing a base model’s pretraining on a focused corpus—here, four versions of the Bible and more than ten Christian public-domain works—so its instincts shift toward that domain before any instruction tuning.
