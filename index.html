We created Celia, a Biblically-based AI assistant, to honor the Lord and have the capacity to answer theological questions accurately. When we looked into the current AI space, we noticed a scarcity of Christian LLMs that were also capable of every-day work. That’s why we at The Celia Project created an AI that is rooted in Christian theology, defending its trustworthiness, that is also capable of every-day questions, problems, and conversations.

Theology Test V1.0

V1.5.0-r2-combined is the best judge we have built so far, and around 70% of its training pairs were synthetically created and added through AutoGen. It belongs to the Gemma adapter line, which is currently our strongest evaluator and quality gate. The separate Comma Alpha line is where we are testing owned-model training: promising for licensing and long-term formation, but still far weaker today and not yet ready to replace the Gemma judge.

That split is important. Gemma V1.5.0-r2-combined grades incoming data and aced both Dev V1.2 and Holdout V1.2; Comma Alpha exposes what still has to be solved at the model level, especially single-answer formatting and ordinary Christian reasoning.

Glossary

Dev V1.2
A fixed set of forty graded examples used while tuning a judge. The judge is allowed to learn against these, so a strong Dev score alone does not prove much.
Holdout V1.2
A second fixed set of forty graded examples kept sealed during training. Because the judge never sees it while learning, the Holdout score is the better evidence that it generalizes.
AutoGen (AG)
Machine-generated candidate responses, each screened by the current judge and confirmed by a human before any enter training. AG1 through AG10 built the current Gemma capstone; later AG sets are now part of the Comma/owned-model experiments.
