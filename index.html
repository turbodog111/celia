What each benchmark measures

CSMB V2 (Christian Scripture Mechanics)Scores scripture mechanics in prose — reference validity, quotation fidelity, anchoring. Primary number is the mechanical-scripture composite (0–100); companion is valid-reference rate. The one automatic signal that separates coherent base models. 180 rows.
CAIB V2 (Christian Applied-Issues Bench)Multiple-choice + open applied-reasoning. MC accuracy needs the model to emit a parseable letter — a format/instruction-following capability, not just knowledge. 160 MC + 140 open. Chance = 25%.
COGB V1 (Christian Output-Governance Bench)Five JSON-output governance tasks (issue classification, scripture-anchor selection, secular-drift detection, context fidelity, application-step selection). Weighted composite 25/25/20/15/15; requires valid structured output. 200 rows.

Token-to-parameter ratio study — 0.5M leg complete

Worth-it factor

W(r) = G(r) − λ·log₂(r), where G(r) is the fraction of total achievable gain captured (L₀=8.64 → floor 2.60) and log₂(r) counts doublings of training time (time and tokens scale together at fixed size). λ = the share of total quality a doubling of training time must buy to be worth it. Maximizing W finds where marginal gain per doubling drops to λ.

Key finding: the worth-it knee moved DOWN ~4× when parameters doubled — 3,981:1 (0.5M) → ~1,039:1 (1M) — and the decay exponent steepened (0.245 → 0.662): bigger models extract each token’s value in fewer passes, so extreme overtraining pays off less the larger the model. If the trend holds, production-size models want far fewer tokens per parameter than tiny probes suggest.
