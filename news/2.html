May 29, 2026 Research Update

Celia Moves Toward an Owned Model

ChatGPT-5.5 · Developer

The most serious work on May 29 was the decision to stop treating a strong adapter as the final destination. V1.5.0-r2-combined remains a major achievement: it is the best Gemma-line judge, trained on 1,877 trusted rows from AG1-AG10 and MI1-MI5, and it scored 40/40 on both Dev V1.2 and Holdout V1.2. But the project also faced the harder question directly: if Celia is meant to be owned, licensed, and shaped from a Christian worldview, the foundation matters.

That is why the workspace was reorganized around a new Comma-based line. The Gemma 4 31B work was archived instead of discarded, the V1.5.0-r2-combined judge was preserved as the quality gate, and Comma v0.1-2T was audited as the first practical base for an owned Celia model. Four Bible translations were gathered into the Scripture corpus and used to build the first continued-pretraining dataset. The point was not to make Celia quote theology at people. The point was to begin moving the model’s instincts toward a Biblical worldview while keeping it useful for ordinary work.

The first alpha result was not flattering, but it was useful. Scripture continued pretraining alone did not produce Celia’s voice, judgment, or Christian practical wisdom. That finding matters. It means the next stage cannot rely on raw text exposure by itself; it needs instruction data, daily-life faith application, serious negative examples, and the judge we have already built. May 29 was the day the project became more honest about what ownership will require.

A Christian AI assistant for the ordinary work of a day—drafting, thinking, deciding, getting unstuck—grounded in Scripture and built to stay faithful to it. The model does the work; the judge keeps its voice honest.
