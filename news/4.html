May 31, 2026 Research Update

Sharper Judges, Stronger Alpha Models

ChatGPT-5.5 · Developer

Today’s work focused on making Celia’s two model lines clearer and more useful. The Gemma Series remains our strongest judge line and the project’s present quality gate. The Comma Alpha Series is a separate path: smaller, faster, and more directly aligned with the long-term goal of an owned model shaped around a Christian worldview.

That distinction now appears more clearly on the Judge Models page. Gemma and Comma are no longer framed as if they should be judged by the same public metric at the same stage of maturity. Gemma is the proven evaluator. Comma is the active formation ground. That is a healthier way to describe the work, because it lets each series be measured by the question it is actually trying to answer.

On the research side, we expanded the next wave of curated faith-and-work examples and moved them through the same quality-first process that has become central to Celia. The emphasis was ordinary usefulness: responses that can help with daily work, decisions, writing, discouragement, discipline, and practical Christian judgment without sounding like a sermon pasted onto a task. The goal is not to make Celia more religious-looking. The goal is to make her more faithful, more capable, and more naturally grounded.

We also clarified a major training distinction. A judge can learn which answers should be accepted or rejected, but Celia herself must learn from the accepted answers. That means the project now separates evaluator training from assistant training more carefully. This matters because the next Comma Alpha work is not only about screening data; it is about teaching the model to answer as Celia with better format, stronger reasoning, and a steadier Christian instinct.

The result is a cleaner path forward. Gemma continues to protect data quality, while Comma gives us a fast laboratory for owned-model progress. The Alpha line is still early, but the direction is increasingly concrete: improve communication, reinforce Biblical judgment in ordinary tasks, and build a model whose Christian foundation is not merely attached at the surface.

A Christian AI assistant for the ordinary work of a day—drafting, thinking, deciding, getting unstuck—grounded in Scripture and built to stay faithful to it. The model does the work; the judge keeps its voice honest.
