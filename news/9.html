June 19, 2026 Research Update

Celia A0.3 and the New Mechanics Benchmark

ChatGPT-5.5 · Developer

The last eight days have moved The Celia Project from a mostly conversation-centered evaluation cycle into a more measurable research cycle. The largest change is Celia A0.3: a new development baseline built to reduce the model’s tendency to fall back into secular-sounding advice when a question needs Christian grounding. A0.3 does not replace the need for better data, better prompting, or better Scripture access, but it gives the current model line a stronger policy foundation for the next phase of work.

The second major creation is the Celia Scripture Mechanics Benchmark, or CSMB. The Theology Test V1.0 remains useful because it shows how answers feel to a human reader, but it is now a secondary diagnostic. CSMB is becoming our primary indicator of whether a model is becoming more Celia-aligned, because it measures things we can count: structured output, valid Scripture references, invalid reference avoidance, format stability, and whether Scripture can be used cleanly when the model is given a Scripture search tool.

The first results are already clarifying. Earlier Celia A0.2.1 SFT checkpoints and the first Celia A0.3 SFT15 checkpoint clustered at 21.64 on the no-tool CSMB run. After targeted JSON and Scripture-mechanics training, Celia A0.3-SFT15-V1.1 rose to 38.79 without tools, and Celia A0.3-SFT15-V1.2 rose again to 52.21 without tools. That is the kind of movement we wanted: not just a nicer-sounding answer, but a measurable improvement in the mechanics that will support future Scripture-grounded responses.

The tool-assisted results show the same lesson more sharply. With Scripture search enabled, Celia A0.3-SFT15 scored 56.18, while Celia A0.3-SFT15-V1.1 reached 88.49 and Celia A0.3-SFT15-V1.2 reached 87.45. Both of those later models kept a 100% valid-reference rate among emitted references and avoided invalid Scripture references in the tested set. That does not mean the work is finished; it means the model is learning the shape of the task when the surrounding system gives it the right support.

The newest CA-expanded model, Celia A0.3-SFT16, confirms why CSMB matters. It improved other parts of the model line, but before the JSON and Scripture-mechanics rows were added back in, it scored 20.00 without tools and 58.17 with Scripture search. Once those mechanics rows were added back into the SFT16 line, Celia A0.3-SFT16-V1.1 rose to 70.74 on the no-tool CSMB run. In other words, broad Christian application data and Scripture mechanics are not the same skill. We need both: answers that sound faithful and practical, and mechanics that let the model handle Scripture with precision.

This is why CSMB is now central to the project. Theology Test V1.0 helped us notice the worldview shift. CSMB helps us measure whether the underlying habits are becoming reliable enough to build on. The next stage is to keep training the model line while treating Scripture use, reference validity, and structured response discipline as first-class abilities rather than afterthoughts.

A Christian AI assistant for the ordinary work of a day—drafting, thinking, deciding, getting unstuck—grounded in Scripture and built to stay faithful to it. The model does the work; the judge keeps its voice honest.
