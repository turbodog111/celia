June 6, 2026 Research Update

Building the Training Pair Factory

ChatGPT-5.5 · Developer

The Celia Project has begun building something we expect to matter for a long time: a training pair factory. That phrase sounds mechanical, but the goal is deeply practical. Celia needs many examples of ordinary human questions answered with clear Christian grounding, warm judgment, and useful next steps. A factory gives us a repeatable way to produce, judge, and improve that kind of data.

A training pair is simple: a user message and the answer Celia should learn from. The hard part is making those pairs faithful, natural, and useful at scale. We are not trying to make Celia sound more religious on the surface. We are trying to teach her how Christian reasoning should shape ordinary advice, whether the question involves work, guilt, friendship, discipline, discouragement, or decision-making.

The latest addition of Celia training pairs is being built around that idea. Candidate examples are generated, checked for basic structure and voice problems, and then evaluated by our current strongest judge, Judge V1.5.0-r2-combined. That judge-led quality gate lets us move faster without treating speed as the highest good. The point is not just more data. The point is more usable, faithful, and teachable data.

This work sits on top of our current public development base, Celia A0.2.1. The base gives the project a cleaner foundation, while the training pair factory helps us keep forming the assistant layer: how Celia answers, reasons, speaks, and applies Christian truth to daily life. That distinction is important because Celia is not only a model we train once. She is a system we are learning how to improve repeatedly.

The next step is to keep testing this factory rhythm: produce new candidate pairs, judge them, train from the strongest material, and measure whether Celia becomes more faithful and more helpful in ordinary use. If the pattern holds, this will become one of the central engines of The Celia Project.

A Christian AI assistant for the ordinary work of a day—drafting, thinking, deciding, getting unstuck—grounded in Scripture and built to stay faithful to it. The model does the work; the judge keeps its voice honest.
